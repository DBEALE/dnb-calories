At heart, it predicts what comes next.

A large language model repeatedly answers one question: given everything so far, what piece of text is most likely to come next?

First, words become tokens.

Models do not see letters or words exactly as we do. Text is split into reusable chunks called tokens, then each token is replaced with an ID number.

03

Numbers gain a sense of meaning.

A token ID alone is arbitrary. An embedding turns it into a long list of numbers: a location in a many-dimensional “meaning space.” Similar ideas tend to land nearby.

04

Attention connects the clues.

To understand each token, the model looks back at other tokens and decides which ones matter most right now. That dynamic lookup is called self-attention.

08

Fluent does not always mean factual.

An LLM learns patterns in language, not a guaranteed model of truth. Knowing its limits is as important as knowing its machinery.

?

Hallucination

It can produce a plausible continuation that is confidently wrong. Important claims should be checked against reliable sources.

▣

Finite context

It only works with the text inside its current context window. Very long conversations may lose or compress older details.

≈

Pattern, not intent

It can imitate reasoning and emotion through language patterns, but that is not evidence of human-like experience or intention.

⚖

Bias in, bias out

Patterns and blind spots in training data can influence outputs. Safety training reduces this risk but cannot erase it entirely.

The one-sentence version

An LLM is a giant pattern-learning machine that generates language one token at a time.

It turns text into numbers, uses attention and learned weights to understand context, then repeatedly predicts what should come next.
